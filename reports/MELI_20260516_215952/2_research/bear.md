### 1. The "Strategic Investment" or "Leverage Trap"?
You call the margin compression a "strategic maneuver," but let’s look at the balance sheet. You pointed to $12.1 billion in operating cash flow, which is impressive, but you ignored the explosive growth in debt. Total debt jumped from $6.85 billion in 2024 to $11.39 billion in 2025. In a stable, low-interest environment, that’s aggressive growth. In the current 2026 macro climate, where recessionary fears and energy costs are rising, that debt is a ticking time bomb. If their logistics investments don't yield immediate, massive returns, that debt service becomes a drag that will choke off any potential recovery. You’re calling it "fuel for the future"; I’m calling it "financial overextension."

### 2. The PEG Ratio Fallacy
You leaned heavily on the PEG ratio of 0.99 to claim the stock is "undervalued." That number is only as good as the growth *expectations* built into the denominator. The market is currently signaling via the price action (trading at $1546, well below the 200 SMA of $2057) that it *does not believe* those growth forecasts are guaranteed.

If the broader economy hits a recession—which is exactly what the market is pricing in—that "G" in your PEG ratio is going to evaporate. When growth slows, high-multiple stocks like MELI don't just correct; they get crushed because the valuation relies entirely on the assumption of uninterrupted, exponential expansion. You are pricing in perfection in an imperfect, volatile macroeconomic landscape.

### 3. Institutional "Smart Money" is Selling
You argued that the institutional "Buy" ratings mean smart money is holding. Let’s look at the volume data instead of the analyst notes.

The VWMA (Volume Weighted Moving Average) is at 1661.24, which is significantly *above* the current price of 1546.81. This is the smoking gun that you’re missing. If "smart money" were accumulating, we would see the VWMA trending downward with or below the price, or we’d see support forming. We aren't. Institutional investors are offloading, and retail is trying to hold the line.

You see an RSI of 34 and think "coiled spring." I see an RSI of 34 and think "downward momentum." In technical analysis, the market can stay oversold longer than you can remain solvent. The MACD is at -55.98 with no sign of a crossover. This isn't a "coiled spring"; it’s a stock that has lost its structural support.
